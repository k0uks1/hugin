A functor is a module parameterised by a record of types and relations, its signature. The standard library's std/graph declares the signature graph and the functor tc, the transitive closure of any graph; its two rules are shown in the comment at the top of the program.

Applying a functor to a record makes an instance: roads is the closure of the road graph, and its relation is roads.path. A relation is an ordinary value of the record: back closes the relation both.link of another instance.

The proofs in Part 2 are mostly about functors, not instances. A theorem about tc G, for a variable G, holds for every graph, and so for every instance.

The rules of a functor's body are named by their head relation and their position: link.1 is the first rule with head link, link.2 the second. Part 2 uses these names to take derivations apart.
