Every theorem in this tour is about the semantics, derives and the perfect model. That a run of the engine prints exactly those facts is one statement for all programs, adequacy, and it is trusted: the engine is tested against the semantics, not verified.

hugin run --certify checks adequacy for a single run. The compiler reifies the rules the engine ran, the input facts and the output, with a derivation of every fact, and the checker of std/certify, written and proved in Hugin, checks them: every printed fact has a derivation (soundness), and the printed facts are closed under the rules (a model check, which gives completeness). An accepted run prints a verdict on the error output (the first line on the right); a rejected one is an error.

What remains trusted after a certified run is the reification: that the rules handed to the checker are the rules of the program as written and the ones the engine ran. The checker itself, its proof against derives and the perfect model, and the meta-level type checker that checks that proof are what the guarantee rests on.

The certified fragment has atoms over variables and constants, negation and comparisons, without aggregates. Checking is quadratic in the number of facts, so it is a mode for tests and audits of small and medium runs. The playground runs the program without --certify; the output on the right is the CLI's, checked in CI.

What this tour has shown

  • Proved by the compiler: transitivity and symmetry of closures, the equivalence of two programs, the soundness of an abstraction, a property of a negated relation, and ground facts by evaluation, each for every graph and every input database where the statement says so.
  • Trusted: adequacy of the engine (checked per run by --certify), the reification of rules (programOf, and the rules --certify hands to the checker), the trusted primitives of the meta level, and the type checker.
  • Not covered: running time, floats and string concatenation in rules (not interpreted by the semantics), and the facts of one particular input unless a lemma takes them as a hypothesis.

The reference has the details: std/semantics, std/strata, std/certify and std/quote.
